What this demonstrates

Build chains, branches, rings and bridges; inspect neutral electron counts and formal charges; identify a simplified donor–H···acceptor motif; and watch incoming energy become stored heat, emitted light or cooling loss. Electrons orbiting the drawing are symbolic decoration, not quantum trajectories. The photon count is an energy-equivalent expectation, not individually simulated photons.

The graph does not determine the energy efficiency. The energy ledger uses an independently adjustable budget. This avoids treating an arbitrary “coherence” score as physical evidence.

Hydrogen → helium · nuclear budget playground

This separate panel assumes completed stellar proton–proton chains. Each completion converts four hydrogen nuclei into one helium-4 nucleus through intermediate reactions; positrons and neutrinos are involved. It is not a chemical bond operation or a prediction of fusion onset.

Approximate total: 26.7 MeV per completed chain, including positron annihilation. The 2.2% neutrino default and user-selected light/heat split are illustrative. Hermes’ Loss · declared attack worksheet (draft adaptation)
Write one measured attack per line: family, change Δ, magnitude a, sensitivity S, counterexample yes/no. Values must share a declared normalization; this tool does not measure them for you.
 
Draft transcriptions: Hᵢ=Δᵢ/aᵢ; Hᵢ*=Δᵢ/(aᵢSᵢ); D=max Hᵢ*; resistance=1/(1+D); coverage=distinct tested families/declared families. The source calls the resistance score “Hermes Loss”; higher resistance is better here. Empty tests remain UNTESTED, not perfect resistance. A counterexample is a user-reported flag and stays visible regardless of the score. These results establish neither truth nor physical validity.
The other graphic describes a different weighted objective (lower is better): Lpred + λLrel + μLcons + ΩLprov. It is not the resistance score.

Hermitian phase lab · your two constructions
Use the current scene atoms to compare consistent node phases with independent pairwise phases. Phases are simulated, in radians—not measured optical signals. This separate lab does not change the historical Stage 5 engine or add energy.
 
Build a scene with 1–24 atoms, then run.
Each colored cell shows a wrapped phase in radians; a dot means no coupling under the bond mask. Hermitian means reverse entries are complex conjugates. PSD means eigenvalues are nonnegative within numerical tolerance. Consistent full node phases give rank one and trace equal to atom count. Pairwise phases can frustrate loops. A tree has no loops to test. Masking bonds preserves Hermitian symmetry but generally loses PSD; that is valid for a coupling matrix, not a density matrix.

Easy start · how to use this
  1. Start with water: choose Water pair and press Load preset. Dashed cyan lines show possible hydrogen-bond motifs.
  2. Add an atom: choose its letter, select Add / move, then click an empty part of the drawing. Drag atoms to rearrange them.
  3. Join atoms: select Bond, choose the bond order, then click the two atoms. Solid lines are the bonds you proposed.
  4. Remove something: select Remove in the Interaction menu, then click an atom or solid bond. An atom takes its attached bonds with it. Clear starts a new blank scene.
  5. Inspect: select Inspect and click an atom to see its electron count and change its formal charge.
  6. Try energy: press Run. Power supplies energy; Light allocation sends a fraction to light; Cooling carries heat away. Pause stops the simulation. Reset energy clears its totals.
  7. Compare the old rule: enter initial/final coherence and stress below the drawing, then press Calculate comparison. These hypothetical scores are separate from the energy budget.
  8. Experiment freely: Undo reverses the last editable scene snapshot; Redo brings it back. Both pause the simulation. Try Double disulfide scaffold to see two C–S–S–C cross-links; these are editable graph proposals, not permanent locks. Use the separate hydrogen-to-helium panel for assumed nuclear energy accounting.
  9. Set the environment: enter starting kelvin and heat capacity, then Apply & reset energy. Try 273.15 K (0°C) or 310.15 K (37°C). Increasing capacity makes the same retained energy cause a smaller temperature rise.
  10. Use Hermes: declare your benchmark and enter measured attack results. Calculate, read the counterexample status, and export the worksheet. Blank attack rows give UNTESTED. It is a worksheet, not an automatic adversarial test runner.
  11. Explore the engine stages: build a small scene, select a stage, and run the baseline/perturbation comparison. For central-atom geometry, use one center and 1–4 peripherals (bent/linear allow 2). Read the source limitations beneath the panel before interpreting scores. Export engine run keeps both results and their declared inputs.
  12. Compare phase constructions: load a ring or build atoms, choose consistent node phases or independent pairwise phases, and press Build phase matrix. Toggle the bond mask to compare full matrices with your graph. Check Hermitian symmetry, eigenvalues and loop closure; Export phase run preserves the actual matrix and its seed.
  13. Keep your work: Export scene saves a JSON file. Import restores it. Export before loading a preset or pressing Clear, because those replace the current scene.
